How it's built

Claude does the reasoning. Public data does the rest.

  1. Live public dataOpen federal notices from the SAM.gov daily extract, Miami-Dade County's own bid feeds, Florida state solicitations, Grants.gov, and USAspending award history.
  2. An agent with 7 toolsClaude decides which to call: search contracts, read one, find competitors, find expiring work, search grants, save to the shortlist, propose an email.
  3. Action, with approvalThe buyer email can be saved as a Gmail draft (one click, after approval).
  4. Text relayTexting runs through a personal AI agent that edits a shared doc, which govbid reads and answers in 5 to 10 minutes. Next: a govbid Twilio number.

Business model

Search is free. The agent working for you is not.

The agent works for you

Matched contracts in your trade every week, written questions and bid paperwork drafted for you to review and send, a buyer email that can be saved as a Gmail draft (one click, after approval), and a warning before work you could take over expires.

A flat subscription, never a cut of the contract: federal rules (FAR 52.203-5) and Miami-Dade and Florida law restrict fees that depend on winning. govbid drafts; the owner approves and sends. We never contact the government, so it is not lobbying. GovTribe lists $1,900 a year for one seat on the GovTribe plan, and $2,100 on Pro. GovSpend and GovWin don't publish a price. General personal agents like Instinct do errands for anyone; this one does one job, government contracting, for the businesses those tools ignore.

How we roll it out

Start by hand. Then let it run.

  1. Month 1We run the agent ourselves for 10 Miami contractors, review every draft with them, and show each one real contract. Free.
  2. Month 2–3The ones who bid become the first $99 subscribers. We track bids submitted and contracts won.
  3. ThenSelf-serve: a contractor signs up with their trade and zip code, and the agent runs on its own with their approval.

Impact

We count one thing: dollars won by local businesses.

Every contract a Miami small business wins is public money that stays in Miami: payroll, jobs, and a track record that makes the next bid easier.

It points every business to free local help: the Florida APEX Accelerator at FIU, Miami-Dade SBE certification, and the county supplier portal. The agent answers in the owner's own language: English, Spanish or Haitian Creole. Next: City of Miami, Miami-Dade schools and Water & Sewer feeds.
